Long-term Planning

The financial
decisions that aren't
really about money.

The numbers matter. But the decisions behind them are usually about something much bigger: your life, your family and what you want the future to look like.

Think further.
Plan wider.

Look beyond the products

Most financial goals
aren't really financial.

People rarely build pensions simply because they want a pension.

They build them because one day they want the freedom to stop working.

People don't necessarily invest because they want an investment portfolio. They may want security, choices, a comfortable retirement, the ability to help their family or simply more control over what happens next.

Money is part of the plan. It isn't usually the reason for it.

Money helps.
Life decides.

Start with life

What do you want
your money to make possible?

Long-term financial planning begins by looking beyond individual pensions, investments and savings accounts.

Those things matter, but they are resources.

The more important questions are often about what you want those resources to help you do.

That could mean retiring earlier, travelling more, moving abroad, reducing your working hours, helping your children or simply knowing that you have enough.

Bigger questions

What are you
planning towards?

The answer is different for everyone, which is why the plan needs to begin with the person rather than the product.

When would you like to stop working? What would you do with more time? Where would you like to live? Who would you like to help? What do you want to leave behind? What would financial freedom mean to you?

Money and time

More isn't always
automatically better.

It is easy to assume that the financial objective should always be to accumulate more.

A larger pension. A larger investment portfolio. Another year of earnings.

Sometimes that is entirely appropriate.

But there comes a point where the question may need to change from “How much more can I build?” to “What is the money already there for?”

Time is also a resource — and unlike money, we cannot accumulate more of it for later.

Retirement

Retirement isn't simply
a date on a pension statement.

Retirement is a lifestyle change.

It can affect your income, spending, routine, family life, location and the way you spend your time.

That means a retirement plan needs to consider more than the value of your pension.

It needs to consider what you expect life after work to cost and whether your overall financial resources may be able to support it.

Family

Your financial plan rarely
affects only you.

Many long-term decisions involve other people.

You may want to help children or grandchildren, support a partner, provide money towards a home or education, or eventually pass wealth to the next generation.

Those ambitions can sit alongside your own retirement and lifestyle needs.

The challenge is understanding how much support may be affordable without compromising your own financial security.

Finding the balance

Enjoy today.
Protect tomorrow.

Long-term planning is often about finding a sensible balance between the life you're living now and the future you're preparing for.

Now matters.
Later does too.

Plans change

Twenty years is
a long time.

A long-term financial plan cannot assume that your life will remain exactly as it is today.

Careers change. Relationships change. Families grow. People move countries. Priorities shift.

Financial markets and economic conditions also change, as can tax and pension rules.

The objective therefore isn't to produce one perfect plan and follow it blindly forever.

It is to create a useful framework that can be reviewed as your circumstances develop.

Ask what if

A plan should survive
a few questions.

Exploring different scenarios can help show how changes in your life might affect the longer-term picture.

What if I retire earlier? What if I move abroad? What if my spending changes? What if I help my family financially? What if investment returns differ? What if my priorities change?

Investments

The portfolio should
serve the plan.

Investment decisions make more sense when they have a clear purpose.

Your goals, timescale, circumstances and attitude to risk all provide context for the way money is invested.

Rather than allowing investments to become the financial plan, they can then take their place as one component within it.

Life across borders

Sometimes the future
isn't in the same country.

Long-term plans may include moving overseas, returning to the UK or retiring somewhere completely different.

International lives can add another layer to financial planning because assets, pensions, income and future goals may cross jurisdictions.

The geography may change. The principle remains the same: understand where you are and where you're trying to get to.

Keep talking

The best plan is one
you're allowed to change.

Financial planning should not lock you into decisions made years earlier.

Regular reviews create an opportunity to ask whether the plan still reflects your life, whether assumptions need changing and whether new priorities have emerged.

Sometimes the numbers change. Sometimes you change.

Both are valid reasons to revisit the plan.

The bigger picture

A good financial plan
isn't really about money.

It's about choices.

When you can stop working. Where you can live. What you can experience. Who you can help. And how confident you can feel about the future you're creating.

The money provides the resources. Your life provides the reason.
