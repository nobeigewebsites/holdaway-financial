More than a set of numbers

Where are you now?
Where could you be heading?

One of the most useful things financial planning can do is help you understand whether the money you have today may support the life you want tomorrow.

Lifetime cashflow modelling brings the different parts of your financial life together and projects them forward over time.

Instead of looking at a pension, investment or savings account in isolation, you can begin to see how everything might work together.

The bigger picture

A pension value doesn't
tell you when you can retire.

Knowing the value of your pension or investments is useful, but on its own it doesn't necessarily answer the questions that matter most.

Can you afford to retire? Could you stop working earlier? How much income might you need? What happens if your circumstances change?

Those are questions about your life, not simply about an account balance.

Lifetime cashflow modelling is designed to put those figures into context.

Start with the life

What are you actually
planning for?

Before modelling the future, it helps to know what that future is supposed to look like.

Your goals provide the context for the numbers.

When would you like to stop working? What income might you need? Where do you want to live? What do you want retirement to look like? Do you want to help your family? Are there major future purchases or plans?

Building the picture

Bringing everything together.

A cashflow model can bring together information about your income, expenditure, pensions, investments, savings, property and other assets.

It can also take account of future events and expected changes.

That might include retirement, receiving a pension, selling a business or property, providing financial support to family or changing the amount you expect to spend.

The aim is not simply to collect financial information. It is to understand what those resources could mean over the course of your life.

The useful bit

Then we can ask:
“What if?”

A financial plan is based on assumptions, and life rarely follows a perfectly straight line.

That is why one of the most valuable parts of cashflow planning is being able to explore different scenarios.

Instead of treating the first projection as an answer carved in stone, we can change individual assumptions and look at the potential effect.

What if...

I retire earlier?

See how stopping work sooner could affect your projected position.

What if...

I spend more?

Explore whether a different lifestyle could remain sustainable.

What if...

Markets perform differently?

Test how changes in assumptions could affect the longer-term picture.

What if...

Life changes?

Update the plan when your circumstances, priorities or goals change.

Retirement planning

When is enough,
actually enough?

Retirement is one of the clearest examples of where lifetime cashflow modelling can help.

People can spend decades building pensions and investments without ever having a clear answer to a much more fundamental question: when can I afford to stop?

The largest possible pension fund is not necessarily the objective.

The objective may be having enough to support the lifestyle you want without spending unnecessary years continuing to work simply because you don't know whether you can afford not to.

One important distinction

A projection isn't
a prediction.

Cashflow modelling cannot tell us exactly what the future will look like.

Plan.
Review.
Adapt.

Investment returns, inflation, tax rules, spending and personal circumstances can all change.

A cashflow model therefore depends on the assumptions used to build it.

Its value is not in pretending that we can predict every detail of the next twenty or thirty years.

Its value is in giving us a framework for making informed decisions today and something we can continue to review as reality unfolds.

Keep it alive

Your financial plan
should move with you.

A lifetime cashflow model becomes much more useful when it is reviewed and updated rather than created once and forgotten.

Ultimately

Financial planning is about
what your money lets you do.

A lifetime cashflow model can help turn a collection of pensions, investments and savings into a clearer picture of what your financial future might look like.

Because sometimes the most useful financial question isn't “How much do I have?” It's “What could I do with it?”

Cashflow modelling uses assumptions about future events including investment returns, inflation, income and expenditure. Projections are illustrative rather than guaranteed and should be reviewed as circumstances and assumptions change.
